QUEST 3 · FERMILABIN PROGRESS

WHY QUANTUM

At Fermilab I learned that one of the biggest costs of a fault tolerant quantum computer is distilling magic states, and that cutting that cost is a search problem. I attacked it with parallel searches on a compute server, and one of my codes beat the published result. A small change in γ means fewer physical qubits for the same computation. That was the moment it clicked. Quantum computing is the hardest scientific computing problem I know, and I can actually move it forward. The full run is in the boss level below.

REWARD · γ 1.7797, BETTER THAN THE PAPER
QUEST 4 · ON MY OWNIN PROGRESS

LEARNING THE WHOLE STACK

After Fermilab I wanted to see every layer between a noisy qubit and a correct answer. So I built them myself. A repetition code simulator, zero noise extrapolation, transpiling circuits onto real IBM device layouts, a C++ matrix library for the linear algebra underneath, and a data pipeline that finds the surface code threshold from 1.8 million decoded shots.

WORLD 4 · FERMILAB SQMS CENTER
QUDIT MAGIC STATE DISTILLATION

At the Superconducting Quantum Materials and Systems Center at Fermilab, a U.S. Department of Energy National Laboratory, I search for punctured Reed Muller triorthogonal codes that make magic state distillation cheaper for qudits. I work with Hank Lamm and Ruth Van de Water and run every search in parallel on Fermilab's Linux compute server.

Once we confirmed the construction is structurally closed for primes up to 13, I moved my search to the open mid k window at p = 7. The internship became a year long independent study.
